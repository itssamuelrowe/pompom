# Pomodoro best practices

The technique is simple, but a few habits make it far more effective.

## Decide what you will do before you start

A pomodoro is for executing, not deciding. Spend a moment choosing one concrete task before you press Start. "Draft the intro section" beats "work on the report." If a task needs more than a few pomodoros, break it down. If it needs less than one, batch it with similar small tasks.

## Protect the focus interval

During a pomodoro, the goal is single-tasking. When a distraction or a new idea pops up, jot it on a scratch list and return to it later rather than switching immediately. The interval is short on purpose, most interruptions can wait 25 minutes.

::: callout
If you are interrupted by something truly unavoidable, it is usually better to reset the pomodoro than to limp through a fragmented one.
:::

## Take real breaks

Breaks exist to let your attention recover, which means stepping away from the work. Stand up, look out a window, stretch, get water. Scrolling a feed keeps your mind in the same "screen" mode and rarely restores focus. Short breaks are for a quick reset. Long breaks are for a proper mental change of scene.

## Tune your durations to the work

Twenty-five minutes is a starting point, not a rule. Deep, flow-friendly work (writing, coding, design) often benefits from longer intervals like 45 to 50 minutes with longer breaks. Shallow or draining tasks may go better in shorter bursts. Use a custom format and adjust until the rhythm feels natural.

::: grid
### Classic

25 min focus, 5 min break, long break every 4. Great default.

### Deep work

50 min focus, 10 min break. Fewer, longer dives for flow states.

### Short sprints

15 min focus, 3 to 5 min break. Good for low-energy or dreaded tasks.
:::

## Use the overtime counter honestly

Sometimes you are mid-flow when the timer ends and you keep going. That is fine. PomPom's overtime counter shows exactly how long you ran over so you can see your real patterns. If you consistently overrun by 10 minutes, that is a signal your pomodoro length could be longer.

## Do not skip the long break

The long break is the part people cut first and regret most. It is what keeps the technique sustainable across a whole day. Treat it as part of the work, not a reward you have to earn.

## Let reminders do the nagging

If you tend to drift off between sessions, enable idle reminders (and the optional idle sound). Turn on funny messages if a little humor helps you actually get back to it. You can also get a notification the moment a session or break ends.
